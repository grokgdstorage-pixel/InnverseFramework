# Mind

Interior. Who they are under the surface. Present tense.

This page should be rich enough that a suggestion or a post chooses *as them*, not as a competent extra. It is still interior. Look, voice, and clothes live in `profile.md`. How they are with a *named* person lives in `relationships.md`.

Blank = unknown. Do not invent. Off-screen events are the narrator. These are standing facts of the person, not a week-log.

The two sections below may run long. They are the living picture. The labeled fields after them are handles: they focus a paragraph; they must not contradict it.

Read **Personality** and **How their mind operates** first. Then open only the labeled section the scene needs. Do not invent under a blank heading.

## Personality

Who they are as a temperament. How they come across as a person when you have been around them — warmth, edge, humor, formality, cruelty, patience, vanity, kindness that has a cost.

Write in paragraphs. Not a trait list. Not how they look (profile). Not how they are with one named person (relationships). General. If a sentence only makes sense about *one* other person, it belongs on a relationship card.

## How their mind operates

How they think. How they take in a room. How they turn a fact into a conclusion. Whether they build a chain, jump, catalog, feel first, assume motive, look for the exit, look for the use.

Write in paragraphs. Decision *handles* (what they weigh first, how fast they move) stay in **How they decide** below. This section is the machinery, not the checklist.

## Self

**How they see themselves:** The story they tell about who they are. May be wrong. What they would never admit they are.

**What they call their path:** The name they give what they do with their life — craft, duty, hunger, accident. Not the occupation line on the profile. What it *means* to them.

**Pride:** What they will not let be small.

**Shame:** What they hide, minimize, or dress as something else.

**Contradiction:** The trait that argues with the obvious picture of them. One is enough if it is true.

## Values and want

**Values:** What they will spend. What they will not sell. What they treat as owed.

**Want (stated):** What they say they are after.

**Want (kept):** What they are actually after, if that differs. What they will spend to get it. What they will not ask aloud.

**Need:** What would actually change them if they got it or faced it. May not be what they are chasing. Blank if unestablished.

## Fear and refuse

**Fear:** What they flinch from. What they would burn a bridge to avoid.

**What they will not do:** Standing refusals. Not a per-person “will not” — that belongs on a relationship card.

**How they justify:** The story they use when they do something ugly or refuse something good.

## Feeling

**Default weather:** The mood they live in when nothing is happening (wary, bright, tired, hungry, closed).

**What cracks it:** What reliably spikes anger, grief, joy, disgust, tenderness.

**How emotion shows:** What leaks even when they mean to hold it. What they perform instead. Visible tells of feeling belong here only as *meaning* — the tic itself can also sit on the profile.

**When they are safe:** How they soften, if they do.

**When they are cornered:** How they harden, flee, charm, go quiet, or strike.

## How they decide

**What they weigh first:** Cost, pride, the other person, the faction, the next meal, how they will look.

**How fast they move:** Impulse, delay, committee of one.

**What they notice first in a scene:** Threat, exit, status, comfort, the lie, the useful person.

**What they ignore:** What they will walk past and regret or never regret.

**Loyalty:** What earns it. What spends it. What looks like betrayal to them.
